2. What Dine Check shows

  • Public inspection records. The directory republishes food-safety inspection results, enforcement findings and hygiene ratings that government food-safety authorities have published. Each listing links to its original source. We transcribe these records carefully, but the original report is the authoritative version.
  • A score is a snapshot. It describes conditions on the day of one visit. Conditions change, and a place with no record has simply not been inspected publicly — that is neither a mark against it nor a clean bill of health.
  • Not official advice. Dine Check is not a government body and does not issue licences or certificates. Nothing on the site is medical, legal or professional advice, and it does not replace the information published by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India or state food-safety departments. A predicted hygiene rating is our own assessment, not an official FSSAI rating.

3. Our own audits

Dine Check audits an establishment only with its agreement, against our published food-safety standard and the scope stated in each report. Our results are a point-in-time assessment by an independent auditor, reviewed before issue. We publish an establishment's results, or share them with partners, only with its written agreement. An establishment may appeal a finding as set out in its report.

4. Asking for an audit

  • A request tells us that you would like a restaurant audited. It is not a complaint or an allegation about the restaurant, and we never present it as one.
  • Sign in with your own Google account and make only genuine requests. One vote per person per restaurant; do not use multiple accounts, automated tools or paid schemes to inflate counts.
  • We decide which restaurants to approach and which requests to show on our public board. We may remove or merge requests that are duplicates, abusive, not about a food business, or otherwise against these terms.
  • A request does not mean the restaurant will be audited. Audits happen only when the establishment agrees.
  • Share pages show a restaurant's name, area and how many people asked. They never show who asked.

5. Restaurants' menu and kitchen pages

  • The restaurant's own information. A menu or kitchen page behind a restaurant's QR code is published by that restaurant. Dishes, prices, descriptions, allergens and nutrition values are entered and checked by the restaurant, not by Dine Check. Prices and availability at the restaurant prevail.
  • Nutrition values are estimates. Calories (kcal) and protein, carbohydrate and fat are worked out from recipes and typical ingredients — sometimes with the help of AI, then checked by the restaurant. They are not laboratory measurements, they are not guaranteed to be accurate, and portions vary.
  • Allergens. Allergens are shown only for dishes the restaurant has checked, and cross-contact is always possible in a kitchen. The allergy filter is a guide, not a guarantee. Always tell the staff about any allergy or dietary need before you order.
  • Kitchen checks show whether the restaurant's own staff recorded their food-safety checks — not the results, and not an inspection, licence or rating.

6. Guest ratings

  • Rate only a place you have visited or ordered from, honestly and in your own words. One rating per phone per restaurant; you may change it. Do not rate a business you own, work for or compete with, and do not offer or accept anything in return for a rating.
  • Do not include links, phone numbers, e-mail addresses, other people's names or personal details, or anything abusive, discriminatory, threatening, obscene, defamatory or untrue.
  • Your rating, comment and any first name you give are public on the restaurant's page. You give Dine Check and the restaurant a free, non-exclusive licence to show them there.
  • Ratings are not checked by Dine Check before they appear and are not a food-safety or hygiene rating. The restaurant may hide a comment that breaks these rules (its stars still count, and the page says how many comments were hidden); it may not hide a comment only because it is critical. We may remove ratings that break these terms, or that look automated or fraudulent.
